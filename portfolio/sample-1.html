Illustrative sample · fictional business

A service page that answers the useful questions.

A worked writing example for a fictional garden-maintenance business, followed by notes on the choices. This is a demonstration, with no client results or performance claims.

The writing brief

A busy homeowner wants help keeping a garden manageable. The page should explain the service, show what needs to be agreed and make asking for a quote straightforward. Service details below are invented for this clearly labelled example.

Start with an offer someone can understand

A vague opening

“We provide exceptional outdoor solutions, transforming spaces with comprehensive excellence and a passion for quality.”

The reader still has to work out what the business does. The draft below names the service and describes a practical process.

An illustrative service-page draft

Fictional garden-maintenance example

Garden maintenance that fits your home and your schedule.

Tell us what needs attention and how often you’d like help. We’ll agree the work before the first visit, so you know what to expect.

Help with the tasks your garden needs

Choose a seasonal tidy-up or discuss recurring visits. The work in your quote can include weeding, light pruning and clearing garden beds. Larger landscaping projects need a separate discussion.

A clear plan before we begin

  1. Send a short description of your garden and the work you have in mind.
  2. We confirm your location, discuss the tasks and arrange any assessment needed.
  3. You receive the agreed work and price before booking.

What is included in a visit?

The tasks confirmed in your quote. Tell us about areas that need particular attention, access requirements and any work you are unsure about.

Can I arrange regular maintenance?

We can discuss a recurring schedule after reviewing the garden and the work needed. Timing and availability are confirmed before you book.

Where do you work?

[Add the actual service area here after the business confirms it.]

Example call to action: Request a garden-maintenance quote

Why these choices help the reader

  • The service is named early. A visitor does not have to translate a slogan into a practical offer.
  • The scope has edges. Recurring maintenance and larger landscaping work are treated as different discussions.
  • The process reduces uncertainty. The customer knows what to send and when the work and price will be agreed.
  • The questions are useful. Scope, recurring visits and service area are details someone may need before enquiring.
  • The next step is specific. Requesting a quote matches the page’s purpose.

A title and description to finish the draft

Title draft: Garden maintenance in [confirmed area] | [Business name]

Description draft: Need help keeping your garden manageable? Discuss a seasonal tidy-up or recurring maintenance in [confirmed area]. Ask for an agreed scope and quote.

The bracketed details are drafting notes. An actual business must confirm the location, tasks, policies and availability before publication. The page would then link to the real enquiry method and any relevant supporting articles.

What this sample demonstrates

It shows how a practical offer, accurate details and a simple next step can fit together. It does not establish a traffic, ranking or enquiry outcome. Those would need real observations with context and permission to share them.
