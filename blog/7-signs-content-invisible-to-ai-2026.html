7 Signs Your Content Needs a Search Refresh

By Harmon Content · Updated 9 October 2026

A confusing page gives a potential customer more work to do. These seven signs are useful reasons to review your content. They are not a test of whether a particular AI system can see or cite your website.

1. The page never says what you actually do

“Complete solutions” could mean almost anything. Read the main heading and opening paragraph on their own. Can someone identify the service and whether it is relevant to them?

A useful fix: name the service, describe who it helps and explain the first step in plain language.

2. Practical details are missing

A visitor may want to know where you work, what is included or what happens when they ask for a quote. If those details are missing, even a well-written introduction leaves the decision unfinished.

A useful fix: gather the questions your customers ask and add confirmed answers in the section where they belong. Do not invent prices, policies or availability.

3. The proof cannot be checked

A large percentage, anonymous praise or a list of credentials is only useful if it is accurate and relevant. A fictional demonstration presented as a case study can leave a visitor with the wrong impression.

A useful fix: use approved, supported evidence. If you are demonstrating writing rather than client results, label the sample clearly and explain the choices. Our illustrative sample does that without claiming a performance outcome.

4. The useful answer is buried

A long opening about the importance of a topic can delay the information a visitor came for. A business owner comparing service options should be able to find the scope without reading a long introduction.

A useful fix: answer the main question early, then add context and qualifications. Keep headings descriptive so a reader can scan the page.

5. Articles lead nowhere

A useful article can still leave a reader unsure how it connects to the business. A resource page with vague “click here” links also makes it harder to choose a destination.

A useful fix: link to the relevant service and related explanations using meaningful words. Google’s SEO Starter Guide explains the role of clear links and logical site organization.

6. Old information still sounds current

A past promotion, an old service limit or an outdated contact method can contradict the rest of the site. Changing only a footer date does not resolve those inconsistencies.

A useful fix: review the visible copy, titles, descriptions and structured data together. Update dates when content is materially reviewed or changed, rather than making old content look new without doing the work.

7. Getting in touch is difficult on a phone

A navigation menu that disappears, a button with hard-to-read text or a form with no visible labels can interrupt an otherwise useful journey.

A useful fix: open the page at a narrow width and follow the path from the offer to the enquiry. Check keyboard focus as well as pointer use. Keep the required fields limited to the information needed to begin a conversation.

Choose one problem to fix first

Start with the issue closest to a customer’s decision. Record what you changed, confirm that the links and enquiry path work, then review the next issue. A focused improvement is easier to maintain than a large pile of new content.
